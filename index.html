Got questions?

The stuff people usually want to know before their first session.

What's the difference between quad skates and inline skates?

Quad skates have four wheels arranged two-by-two, like a classic roller skate — they're generally more stable side-to-side, which makes them the go-to for roller disco, dance, and derby. Inline skates ("rollerblades") have wheels in a single row and suit speed and long-distance skating better. Nearly every session listed on this site is quad-skate focused.

Do I need my own skates, or can I hire a pair?

You can hire. Most beginner-friendly sessions in Leeds — including taster sessions and lessons — offer skate hire for a small extra fee, so there's nothing to buy before your first go. Once you know you're hooked, our gear page has recommendations for a first pair.

Is roller skating hard to learn, and how long does it take?

Most people can stand, push off, and stop with some confidence after two or three sessions. Getting properly comfortable — turning, skating backwards, controlling your speed — usually takes a few weeks of regular practice. Structured lessons (filter by "Lessons" above) get you there faster than going it alone.

Am I too old to start roller skating?

No — adult beginners are the backbone of the Leeds skating scene. Several sessions listed here are aimed specifically at adults, and it's common for most attendees to be 40+. It's never too late to start.

What should I wear to my first session?

Something you can move freely in — leggings or joggers rather than jeans, and socks that come up over your ankle (not too thick, not too thin). Bring a water bottle; most venues warm up fast once everyone's moving.

Do I need a helmet or other protective gear?

It's strongly recommended, especially while you're learning to fall safely. A helmet and wrist guards are the two most worth having from day one, with knee pads a close third. See our gear page for what we'd actually buy.

What's the difference between a roller disco, roller dance, and roller derby?

A roller disco is social skating to music, aimed at all levels. Roller dance is a specific skill discipline — footwork and choreography on skates, usually taught in structured classes. Roller derby is a full-contact team sport on skates. You'll see all three tagged on the events above, so you can filter for whichever you're after.

Where can I actually go roller skating in Leeds?

That's what this site is for — every listing above is a real, upcoming session somewhere in Leeds or West Yorkshire, from weekly roller discos to one-off outdoor meetups. Filter by type or month to find what's on next.
